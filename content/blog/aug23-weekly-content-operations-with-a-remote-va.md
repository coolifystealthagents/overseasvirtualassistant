## Give each day a different operational purpose

Use the start of the week to confirm the active questions and the decisions they support. The assistant can check that each brief has an owner, source boundary, and next deliverable. Items missing one of those elements stay in intake rather than quietly entering research. This keeps Monday from becoming a ceremonial status meeting followed by several days of avoidable ambiguity.

Midweek is for evidence and exceptions. Review source gaps, overlapping angles, access problems, and claims that need owner judgment while there is still time to change direction. Keep the checkpoint short and artifact-based. The discussion should end with an updated brief, a bounded escalation, or permission to continue. A chat acknowledgment without a changed work record is easy to lose across time zones.

Close the week by reconciling the calendar with what actually happened. Accept completed handoffs, return specific revisions, and move unresolved work into a named state. Note one process adjustment for the following week, but avoid changing several rules at once. A stable rhythm helps a remote virtual assistant learn what good work looks like and helps the owner see whether delays come from research, review coverage, or an overloaded queue.
