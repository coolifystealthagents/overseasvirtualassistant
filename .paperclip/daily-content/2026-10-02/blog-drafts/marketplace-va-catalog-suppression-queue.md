# Build a catalog-suppression queue for an ecommerce virtual assistant

A marketplace can hide a product because an image, claim, identifier, safety field, or policy record fails a platform check. The notice may be brief while the listing contains hundreds of fields. A virtual assistant can collect the evidence and prepare a correction queue. The account owner, compliance reviewer, and other qualified people must approve claims, regulated information, appeals, and final listing changes.

The queue should connect one platform notice to the exact product record and evidence used to resolve it. It should not guess what the marketplace meant or change every field in hope that one edit works.

## Capture the platform notice as issued

Record the marketplace, account, listing identifier, product identifier, notice code, full notice text, issue time, source URL, deadline shown, and affected market. Save a controlled screenshot or export when policy permits. Do not rely on a paraphrase copied into chat.

Link the current listing version and recent change history. A suppression that appears after an image update may still relate to another field. Time proximity is a useful clue, not proof of cause. Keep the platform's statement separate from the team's working hypothesis.

If several listings share a notice, create individual cases linked to a parent incident. Each product may have different source documents, owners, or allowed claims. Bulk edits before review can spread an error.

## Trace every questioned claim

Build a field map for title, bullets, description, images, category, identifiers, dimensions, ingredients or materials, warnings, and other attributes used by the channel. For each disputed field, record the listing text, internal source, source date, owner, and review state.

The FTC's advertising guidance explains that advertising claims must be truthful, not misleading, and supported where required. A virtual assistant can locate the approved source and flag a mismatch. The assistant should not decide that evidence substantiates a health, performance, environmental, comparative, or safety claim.

Supplier copy is not automatically approved evidence. A competitor's listing is not evidence at all. When the source is missing or conflicts with the product record, leave the claim unresolved and send it to the named reviewer.

## Separate evidence from speculation

Use three columns: platform-stated issue, observed record, and reviewer question. For example, the platform may state "unsupported attribute." The assistant may observe that the listing says a product is waterproof while the approved specification says water resistant. The reviewer question is whether the listing must be corrected and whether other channels contain the same wording.

This format prevents a plausible theory from becoming the official reason. Marketplaces may run several checks, and their notices may change. Record every portal message and case response in sequence.

Do not tell customer-service staff that a product is unsafe or noncompliant unless an authorized owner has made that determination. If the notice raises a safety concern, route it immediately under the company's safety procedure.

## Work a realistic suppression case

Suppose a marketplace suppresses a kitchen appliance listing with the notice "missing required compliance information." The catalog shows a model number, product images, dimensions, and a certification logo. The product file contains two certification documents, but one names an earlier model suffix.

The assistant records the notice, freezes the listing version, and links both documents. The assistant compares model identifiers without deciding that the earlier document covers the current product. A compliance owner reviews the relationship and rejects the older document for this case.

The owner supplies the approved current record and exact attribute value. The assistant prepares a scoped edit, with before-and-after text and the source link. The account owner approves the change and submission. The queue records the portal confirmation and waits for an actual restoration event rather than treating submission as success.

## Control listing edits and appeals

Every proposed edit should name the field, old value, new value, evidence, requester, reviewer, and rollback value. Group only changes that share the same evidence and approval. Avoid unrelated copy improvements during suppression recovery because they make the result harder to interpret.

An appeal is a representation to the platform. The assistant can assemble the notice, product record, approved evidence, correction history, and draft chronology. The authorized account or compliance owner approves the argument and sends it through the official channel.

Store credentials in approved systems and use individual accounts where the marketplace supports them. Do not give broad payment, user-management, or account-owner permissions merely to update catalog fields.

## Verify restoration and recurrence

Check the listing state through the approved seller system and the public product page where appropriate. Confirm that the intended fields render, images load, the canonical product identifier is correct, and the suppression state has cleared. A portal acknowledgment alone is not restoration.

Record the verification time, market, reviewer, and visible result. If the listing returns and is suppressed again, open a new event linked to the first case. Do not overwrite the earlier evidence.

Pilot the queue with a mix of missing fields, conflicting identifiers, image issues, unsupported claims, and a case that needs an appeal. Review every change during the pilot. Track notices, complete evidence packets, owner corrections, rejected hypotheses, restoration results, and repeat suppressions.

See the [FTC advertising and marketing guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing) for general background. Marketplace rules, product requirements, and applicable law vary; qualified owners must approve the listing's claims and compliance position.
