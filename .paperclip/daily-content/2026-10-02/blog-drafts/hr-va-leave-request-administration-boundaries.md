# Set administrative boundaries for leave-request support by a virtual assistant

An employee's leave request can involve forms, notices, dates, medical documents, payroll coordination, and sensitive conversations. A virtual assistant can receive approved intake information, track required administrative steps, and route questions. Eligibility, designation, accommodation, discipline, and employment decisions must remain with authorized HR staff and qualified advisers.

The workflow needs a clear line between collecting a request and deciding it. A complete intake record does not mean leave is approved. It means the responsible HR person can review the request without searching several inboxes or asking the employee to repeat private information.

## Separate intake from eligibility

Use the employer's approved request channel and notice language. Record the employee identifier, request date, preferred contact method, dates or schedule stated by the employee, type of request in the employee's words, documents received, and assigned HR owner. Avoid asking the assistant to label the request under a law or policy.

The U.S. Department of Labor provides public information about the Family and Medical Leave Act, but an administrative assistant should not decide that a person is eligible or that an event qualifies. Employer size, employee history, location, reason, timing, and other facts can matter. HR applies the policy and obtains advice where needed.

If an employee mentions illness, disability, pregnancy, military service, family care, workplace injury, safety, retaliation, or another sensitive issue, route the exact message promptly. Do not narrow it to the category that seems most obvious.

## Collect only what the process requires

Create a field map showing what the assistant may collect, where it belongs, who may see it, and when it is removed. Medical details should not be copied into a general task description or manager calendar. The tracking board can show that a document was received and who reviews it without exposing its contents.

Use individual accounts, multifactor authentication, restricted folders, and activity logs where available. Do not ask an employee to send medical records to a personal email address. If a document arrives through an unapproved channel, follow the employer's secure-handling procedure instead of forwarding it widely.

When information is missing, request the specific administrative item approved by HR. Do not ask for a diagnosis or additional detail merely to make the file feel complete. Record that the field is pending and the date of the approved follow-up.

## Track notices and dates by source

Every date needs a source. The employee may state a requested start date. A health care provider may state a period on a form. HR may set a response date. Payroll may have a processing cutoff. Store these as separate events rather than one ambiguous "leave date."

The assistant can schedule reminders from dates that HR has confirmed. The assistant should not calculate legal deadlines from memory or change a date because a manager prefers another schedule. If records conflict, preserve both and flag the issue for the HR owner.

Keep the communication history with sender, recipient, timestamp, channel, document reference, and next owner. A sent email is not proof that the employee received or understood a notice. Use the employer's approved delivery and acknowledgment method.

## Work a representative intermittent request

Suppose an employee tells a manager that recurring appointments may require several late starts over the next two months. The manager forwards the message to the approved leave channel. The employee has not named a policy or supplied a schedule.

The assistant opens an intake record, preserves the employee's wording, records the receipt time, and sends the approved acknowledgment and process information. The assistant does not tell the manager that the request is or is not protected. The HR owner reviews the facts and decides which notices or forms apply.

When a restricted document arrives, the assistant records receipt in the administrative tracker and stores the file in the approved HR location. Only the qualified reviewer sees the substantive contents. The employee later changes one appointment date; the assistant records the request and routes any schedule decision under HR's procedure.

## Escalate accommodation and employee-relations language

Employees may not use the employer's preferred terms. A statement such as "I cannot stand for the full shift right now" may require review even if the person never says "accommodation." The assistant should preserve the statement and send it to the named HR owner without interpreting it.

Complaints about treatment, manager pressure, pay, safety, privacy, or retaliation need an immediate route. Do not place them into the ordinary missing-document queue. Do not ask the manager accused in a complaint to decide whether the message matters.

The assistant can acknowledge receipt using approved language. Promises about confidentiality, job protection, approval, or outcome belong to authorized staff. Silence from a busy HR owner does not widen the assistant's authority.

## Audit access and closure

Review every sensitive escalation and sample ordinary records. Compare the tracker with source messages, approved notices, restricted documents, and HR decisions. Look for excess medical detail, unsupported eligibility labels, missed changes, wrong recipients, stale reminders, and files left open after a decision.

Track requests received, packets awaiting employee action, packets awaiting HR action, notices sent, employee corrections, access exceptions, and reviewer changes. State the number of records examined. Do not measure assistants by approval speed because they do not control the decision.

Close a record only after HR confirms the administrative file is complete and assigns retention. Remove temporary copies and revoke access that is no longer needed. Preserve the decision owner and source without exposing sensitive reasoning to unauthorized users.

Pilot the lane with synthetic cases covering a straightforward request, intermittent schedule, incomplete document, accommodation language, privacy error, and employee-relations complaint. HR reviews every action before scope expands.

See the [U.S. Department of Labor FMLA resources](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fmla) for general federal information. Employers must apply the law, policy, contracts, and local requirements relevant to each situation with qualified HR and legal guidance.
